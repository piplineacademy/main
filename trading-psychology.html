Trading Psychology

Your edge is worthless if you can't execute it. These are the eight failures that show up in almost every trading journal.

Trap 1

Revenge trading

You take a loss, feel the sting, and immediately re-enter to win it back — usually bigger and without a setup. The loss stops being money and becomes a personal score to settle.

Countermeasure: Hard-code a cooling-off period: after any loss, no new order for fifteen minutes. After two losses, the day is over. Automate it by logging out.

Trap 2

FOMO

A move takes off without you, and you chase it near the end because missing out feels worse than losing.

Countermeasure: Accept that you will only ever catch a fraction of moves. Keep a 'missed trades' page in your journal and review it monthly — most chases would have been losses.

Trap 3

Overconfidence after wins

Three winners in a row and suddenly your size doubles and your criteria loosen. Winning streaks end more accounts than losing ones.

Countermeasure: Fix size by rule, not by mood. Review your rules on the day after a big win, not after a big loss.

Trap 4

Loss aversion

You cut winners early to lock in a good feeling and let losers run hoping they come back. The maths of this is fatal.

Countermeasure: Set the stop and the target before entry and treat both as untouchable except by a written trailing rule.

Trap 5

Analysis paralysis

Twelve indicators, four timeframes, and no trades taken because something always looks slightly off.

Countermeasure: Reduce to one setup and one pair for a month. Constraints create decisions.

Trap 6

Confirmation bias

You want the trade, so you scroll timeframes until one agrees with you.

Countermeasure: Write your bias down before opening the chart, then actively list the two strongest arguments against it.

Trap 7

Tilt

Frustration, fatigue or something outside trading bleeds into your execution and you stop recognising your own behaviour.

Countermeasure: Score your state 1–5 before each session. Below a 3, you're reviewing charts only, not trading.

Trap 8

Boredom trading

The market is quiet, so you invent a setup to have something to do.

Countermeasure: Have a defined non-trading task for dead sessions: backtest, journal review, or close the laptop.

Pre-session readiness check

Answer honestly. Anything under 70% and you're better off reviewing charts than trading them.

0 of 6 — not ready to trade.
