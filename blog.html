Why your stop keeps getting hit before the move

Stops cluster where everyone can see them: just beyond the obvious swing, on round numbers, at the session high. Liquidity gets taken there because th…

Stops cluster where everyone can see them: just beyond the obvious swing, on round numbers, at the session high. Liquidity gets taken there because that's where it sits. The fix isn't a wider stop — it's a stop placed beyond the noise, with the lot size reduced to keep the money risk identical. Measure the pair's average excursion beyond a swing before it turns, and place your stop past that distance.

The journal entry that actually changes behaviour

Most journals record what happened. Useful ones record what you expected and how you felt. Add two fields to every entry: your confidence out of five before entry, and one sentence about your emotional state. After fifty trades, sort by confidence. Almost everyone finds their high-confidence trades perform no better than their medium ones — which is a direct instruction to stop sizing up on conviction.

Reading a range without getting chopped

Ranges destroy trend traders because every breakout fails. The tell is a contracting range with declining volatility: that's compression before expansion. A range with fat, full-bodied candles hitting both boundaries is a range to fade at the edges. A range with shrinking candles is a range to trade the break of. Same shape, opposite plan.

What one percent risk actually feels like

On a $2,000 account, one percent is twenty dollars — an amount that feels too small to bother with, which is exactly why beginners abandon it. But the rule isn't about the dollar figure; it's about the number of consecutive losses you can absorb. At 1%, ten losses in a row costs under 10% of the account. At 5%, the same streak takes 40% and requires a 67% gain to recover.

Trading the news without trading the spike

The first thirty seconds after a release belong to algorithms with better latency than you. The tradeable opportunity is usually the second move: after the initial spike, price often retraces into the pre-release range and then resolves in the direction of the surprise. Waiting for the first fifteen-minute candle to close gives you a defined range, a defined stop, and no slippage.

Backtesting properly with a bar replay

Scrolling back through a chart teaches you almost nothing because you can see what happened next. Bar replay forces the same uncertainty you face live. Do a hundred trades in replay with a written setup, log every one, and only then decide whether the strategy is worth demo time. It's the cheapest hundred trades you'll ever take.

Correlation: the risk hiding in your open positions

Three longs on EUR/USD, GBP/USD and AUD/USD is one short-dollar position in three costumes. If the dollar rallies, all three lose together. Before adding a position, ask what the common factor is across everything you hold. Cap total exposure to any single currency, not just per-trade risk.

Strategy-hopping is the most expensive habit in retail trading, because no approach gets a sample size large enough to evaluate. Pick one setup, one or two pairs and one session. Take every valid signal for thirty days. At the end you'll have data instead of opinions — and data is the only thing that can tell you to change.
