Lesson 01

What the forex market actually is

Foreign exchange is the simultaneous purchase of one currency and sale of another. There is no central exchange — trades happen through a network of banks, brokers and liquidity providers, which is why the market runs continuously from Sunday evening to Friday evening. Your broker is not the market; it is your doorway to it, and the quality of that doorway matters.

Lesson 02

Reading a currency pair

In EUR/USD the first currency is the base and the second is the quote. The price is how many units of the quote currency one unit of the base costs. Buying EUR/USD means you expect the euro to strengthen against the dollar. Majors contain the US dollar, crosses do not, and exotics pair a major with a smaller economy — the further you go down that list, the wider the spread.

Lesson 03

Pips, points and lots

A pip is the fourth decimal place for most pairs and the second for yen pairs. A standard lot is 100,000 units, a mini lot 10,000 and a micro lot 1,000 — giving roughly $10, $1 and $0.10 per pip on dollar-quoted pairs. Position size is the dial that converts an abstract chart idea into real money, so learn these numbers cold.

Lesson 04

Spread, commission and swap

The spread is the gap between bid and ask, and it is a cost you pay the moment you enter. Commission-based accounts show a rawer spread plus an explicit fee. Swap is the overnight financing charge or credit based on interest-rate differences between the two currencies. Scalpers live and die by spread; swing traders should watch swap.

Lesson 05

Leverage and margin

Leverage lets you control a large position with a small deposit; margin is the deposit itself. Leverage does not increase risk on its own — position size does. A trader on 1:500 leverage risking 0.5% per trade is far safer than one on 1:10 risking 20%. Treat leverage as capital efficiency, never as a multiplier for ambition.

Lesson 06

Order types

A market order fills now at the best available price. A limit order waits for a better price; a stop order triggers at a worse one, used for breakouts and for exits. Every position should carry a stop loss the moment it opens, and a take profit or a written rule for where you'll exit manually.

Lesson 07

Sessions and liquidity

Sydney, Tokyo, London and New York overlap in a predictable rhythm. The London open and the London–New York overlap carry the deepest liquidity and the widest ranges; the late New York and early Asia hours are thin and prone to false moves. Trade the sessions that match the pairs and the volatility you want.

Lesson 08

Fundamentals in one lesson

Currencies move on relative interest-rate expectations, growth, inflation and risk appetite. Central bank policy is the biggest lever: a bank expected to hike tends to see its currency bid well before the hike lands. You do not need to be an economist — you need to know what is scheduled and what the market already expects.

Lesson 09

Building a trading plan

A plan states: the pairs you trade, the sessions you trade, your setup criteria, your entry trigger, your stop and target logic, your maximum risk per trade and per day, and the conditions under which you stop trading. If it isn't written, you don't have one — you have intentions.

Lesson 10

Journaling every trade

Record the setup, screenshot, reason for entry, risk in R, outcome and one sentence on your emotional state. After fifty trades your journal will tell you something no course can: which of your own rules you break, and what it costs you.

Lesson 11

Backtesting and forward testing

Backtesting on historical charts builds pattern recognition cheaply. Replay bar by bar rather than scrolling, so you can't see the future. Then forward test on demo in live conditions — spread, slippage and your own hesitation only show up in real time.

Lesson 12

Going live responsibly

Move to a live account only when you have a written plan, at least a hundred logged demo trades and a flat or rising equity curve. Start at a fraction of your intended size. Your first live goal is not profit; it is executing the plan exactly while real money is on the line.
