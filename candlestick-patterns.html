Candlestick Patterns

Filter by bias or by size, search by name, and click any pattern to see how it forms and when it is worth acting on.

16 patterns shown

neutralsingle-candle

Doji

Open and close are almost identical, leaving a tiny body with wicks on both sides. It signals genuine indecision. On its own it means little; after an extended run into a level, it is often the first hint that the move is running out of participants.

bullishsingle-candle

Hammer

A small body at the top of the range with a long lower wick at least twice the body. Sellers pushed price down and were fully rejected. Meaningful only at the bottom of a decline and at a level you had already marked.

bearishsingle-candle

Shooting Star

The mirror of the hammer: small body at the bottom, long upper wick. Buyers were rejected at a high. Strongest when it forms into prior resistance after a sharp rally.

trendsingle-candle

Marubozu

A full-bodied candle with little or no wick. One side controlled the entire session. A bullish marubozu breaking a range often marks genuine intent rather than a probe.

neutralsingle-candle

Spinning Top

Small body, wicks of similar length on both sides. Momentum is fading. In a strong trend it is usually a pause, not a reversal.

bullishdouble-candle

Bullish Engulfing

A down candle followed by an up candle whose body completely covers it. Demand overwhelmed the previous session. The wider the second body relative to recent candles, the more it says.

bearishdouble-candle

Bearish Engulfing

An up candle swallowed whole by the next down candle. Common at the top of a rally into resistance, especially when volume or range expands.

bullishdouble-candle

Piercing Line

A down candle, then an up candle that opens lower but closes above the midpoint of the first. A partial engulf — weaker than a full engulfing bar but valid at support.

bearishdouble-candle

Dark Cloud Cover

The bearish counterpart: opens above the prior high and closes below the midpoint of the previous up candle.

reversaldouble-candle

Tweezer Top / Bottom

Two or more candles rejecting almost the exact same price. It shows a level being defended twice, which makes it a good place to hang a stop just beyond.

bullishtriple-candle

Morning Star

A long down candle, a small indecisive candle, then a strong up candle closing well into the first body. A classic three-bar bottom that reads like a handover from sellers to buyers.

bearishtriple-candle

Evening Star

The topping version — a strong rally candle, a stall, then decisive selling that erases most of the first candle.

bullishtriple-candle

Three White Soldiers

Three consecutive strong up candles with higher closes and small wicks. Signals sustained buying, though it often arrives late in a move.

bearishtriple-candle

Three Black Crows

Three consecutive strong down candles. Same logic inverted, and same caveat about arriving late.

continuationdouble-candle

Inside Bar

A candle whose entire range sits inside the previous one. Volatility is compressing. Traders often use the break of the mother bar as a trigger in the direction of the trend.

continuationtriple-candle

Rising / Falling Three

A long trend candle, a few small counter-trend candles that stay inside its range, then another long candle in the original direction. A pause, not a turn.

Context beats the pattern. A hammer in the middle of a range is noise. The same hammer at a weekly support level that has already held twice, in a session with real liquidity, is a trade. Always ask what the candle is rejecting before you ask what it is called.
