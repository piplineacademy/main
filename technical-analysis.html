Technical Analysis

How to read a chart in a defined order: structure first, levels second, indicators last.

Market structure

Everything else sits on top of this. An uptrend is a sequence of higher highs and higher lows; a downtrend is the reverse; a range is neither. Mark the swing points on a higher timeframe first, then drop down. The moment a higher low is broken in an uptrend, the structure has changed and your bullish bias needs evidence to survive.

Trade with structure, not against it. Most losing streaks trace back to fading a trend because it "looked extended".

Support and resistance

Levels are zones, not lines. Draw them from candle bodies where price repeatedly turned, and give them a few pips of tolerance. The most reliable levels share three properties: they were respected more than once, they produced a sharp reaction rather than a slow drift, and they are visible on a higher timeframe.

Old resistance frequently becomes new support after a clean break — the "flip" level is one of the highest-quality entries available to a beginner.

Trendlines and channels

Connect at least two swing lows in an uptrend and extend forward. A third touch confirms it. Channels add a parallel line at the opposing swings, framing where a move is stretched. Trendlines are guides for where to look, not automatic entry signals — wait for a candle to react at the line.

Multi-timeframe alignment

Use three timeframes: one for bias (daily or 4H), one for the setup (1H), one for the trigger (15M or 5M). The bias timeframe decides direction, the setup timeframe locates the level, the trigger timeframe gets you in with a tight stop. When they disagree, stand aside — the trade you skip costs nothing.

Moving averages

A moving average is a smoothed view of recent price. The 20 EMA tracks short-term momentum, the 50 tends to act as dynamic support in healthy trends, and the 200 is a widely watched long-term line. Crossovers lag by definition; their honest use is as a trend filter — for example, only taking longs while price holds above the 200.

RSI, MACD and momentum

RSI measures the speed of recent gains against losses on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 or below 30 do not mean reverse; in a strong trend RSI can sit overbought for weeks. Divergence — price making a new high while RSI does not — is the more useful signal, and only at a level.

MACD compares two moving averages and plots the gap. Watch the histogram shrinking into a level rather than the crossover itself.

Volume and volatility

Spot forex has no centralised volume, so tick volume is a proxy for activity, not size. It is still useful: a breakout on a volume spike has more participation behind it than one on a quiet Asian session. ATR measures average range and belongs in your stop placement — a fixed 20-pip stop is meaningless if the pair moves 140 pips a day.

Building confluence

One signal is a guess. Confluence is when independent reasons point the same way: higher-timeframe trend, a flip level, a Fibonacci retracement zone and a rejection candle all in the same place. Require at least three before risking money, and write them down before you click buy — not after.

The order matters. Structure → levels → trigger → risk. Indicators are the last 10% of a decision, never the first.
