Rules worth stealing

  • Risk a fixed fraction. One percent or less per trade, calculated from the current balance, every single time.
  • Cap the day. Two consecutive losses or 3% down — the platform closes. No exceptions, no "one more".
  • Stop first, size second. Place the stop where the idea is invalid, then let the calculator decide the lot size. Never the reverse.
  • Never average a loser. Adding to a losing position converts a planned loss into an unplanned one.
  • Count in R, not dollars. Dollars trigger emotion; R keeps every trade the same size in your head.
  • Correlation is hidden size. Long EUR/USD and long GBP/USD at 1% each is closer to one 2% trade than two independent ones.

Where stops belong

A stop marks the price at which your reason for being in the trade no longer exists — beyond the swing that formed the setup, beyond the level you're trading from, and outside normal noise for that pair. A common approach is to use a multiple of ATR so the distance adapts to current volatility.

Stops that sit exactly on a round number or a visible swing get hunted. Place yours a little beyond the obvious, then reduce lot size to keep the money risk identical.

If widening a stop forces a lot size you're uncomfortable with, the trade is too big for your account — not the stop too wide.
