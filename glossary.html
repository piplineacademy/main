Trading Glossary

Search or jump by letter. Every definition is written to be understood on the first read.

59 terms

Ask

The price at which you can buy. Always the higher side of the quote.

ATR

Average True Range — the average size of recent candles, used to size stops to current volatility.

Backtest

Testing a strategy against historical price to estimate how it would have performed.

Base currency

The first currency in a pair; the one you buy or sell.

Bid

The price at which you can sell. The lower side of the quote.

Breakout

Price moving decisively beyond a defined level or range.

Bullish / Bearish

Expecting price to rise / to fall.

Candlestick

A bar showing open, high, low and close for one time period.

Carry trade

Holding a higher-yielding currency against a lower-yielding one to collect the interest differential.

Confluence

Multiple independent reasons pointing to the same trade idea.

Correlation

The tendency of two instruments to move together or inversely.

Cross pair

A pair that does not include the US dollar, such as EUR/GBP.

Divergence

Price and an oscillator disagreeing — a new price extreme not matched by the indicator.

Drawdown

The decline from an equity peak to a trough, expressed as a percentage.

ECN

Electronic Communication Network — a broker model routing orders to external liquidity providers.

Equity

Account balance plus or minus the floating profit and loss of open positions.

Expectancy

Average profit or loss per trade in R, given a win rate and reward-to-risk ratio.

Exotic pair

A major currency paired with a smaller or emerging economy; wide spreads, thin liquidity.

Fibonacci retracement

Horizontal levels at 38.2%, 50% and 61.8% of a prior move, used to find pullback entries.

Fill

The actual execution of an order, at the price the broker gives you.

Fundamental analysis

Assessing currency value through economic data, interest rates and policy.

Gap

A jump between one candle's close and the next candle's open, common at the Sunday open.

Hedging

Holding offsetting positions to reduce exposure.

Leverage

Borrowed exposure allowing a large position from a small deposit.

Limit order

An instruction to buy below or sell above current price.

Liquidity

How easily an instrument can be traded without moving its price.

Long

A buy position that profits if price rises.

Lot

A standard position unit: 100,000 units of the base currency.

Major pair

A pair including the US dollar and another large economy's currency.

Margin

The deposit your broker holds to keep a leveraged position open.

Margin call

A broker demand for more funds when equity falls below the required margin.

Market order

An instruction to execute immediately at the best available price.

Moving average

The average closing price over N periods, plotted as a line.

Order block

A zone where large orders previously entered, often producing a strong reaction on a retest.

Overbought / Oversold

Oscillator readings suggesting a move has been fast — not a reversal signal by itself.

Pip

The smallest conventional price increment: 0.0001 for most pairs, 0.01 for yen pairs.

Pipette

A tenth of a pip, shown as the fifth decimal place.

Position sizing

Choosing lot size so a stop-loss hit costs a fixed percentage of the account.

Quote currency

The second currency in a pair; the one the price is expressed in.

R-multiple

Profit or loss expressed as a multiple of the amount risked on that trade.

Range

A period where price oscillates between horizontal boundaries with no trend.

Requote

A broker offering a different price because the market moved before execution.

Resistance

A zone where selling has repeatedly overcome buying.

Risk-to-reward

The ratio of potential profit to the amount risked.

Rollover / Swap

Interest credited or debited for holding a position past the daily rollover time.

Scalping

Very short-term trading, typically holding for seconds to minutes.

Short

A sell position that profits if price falls.

Slippage

The difference between your expected and actual fill price.

Spread

The difference between bid and ask — your immediate cost of entry.

Stop loss

A resting order that closes a position at a predefined loss.

Support

A zone where buying has repeatedly overcome selling.

Swing trading

Holding positions for days to weeks to capture larger moves.

Take profit

A resting order that closes a position at a predefined gain.

Technical analysis

Studying price and volume history to anticipate future movement.

Tick

The smallest recorded price change.

Trailing stop

A stop that follows price at a fixed distance, locking in gains as a trade runs.

Trend

A sustained sequence of higher highs and higher lows, or lower highs and lower lows.

Volatility

The size and speed of price movement over a period.

Whipsaw

A sharp move that reverses immediately, stopping out traders on both sides.
